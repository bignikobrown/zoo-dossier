The Raggiana Bird of Paradise

Status/Conservation

  • The raggiana bird of paradise is not threatened, widespread, and relatively tolerant[1] of being around humans.

Full Scientific Classification

  • Scientifically, the raggiana bird of paradise goes by the Paradisaea raggiana.
  • Kingdom: Animalia
  • Phylum: Chordata
  • Subphylum: Vertebrata
  • Class: Aves
  • Order: Passeriformes
  • Family: Paradisaeidae
  • Genus Species: Paradisaea
  • Species: Paradisaea raggiana

Physical Profile

  • Range from 33-34cm in length.
  • Typically may weight about 133-220g
  • Bright and colorful birds, adopting traits of red, green, black, and orange patterns.
  • Can also be identified by their long wings and yellow eyes.

Habitat & Geography

  • Found in the southern and eastern regions of Papa New Guinea, west to the Fly and Strickland Rivers and into the Trans-Fly region in south and west to upper Ramu River and to Gogol River near Madang in north.

Ecology & Behavior

Feeding & Diet:

  • This bird enjoys to eat fruits, figs and arthropods.

Behaviors and Adaptations:

  • Not much is known about the behavior of this species, but it is seen that when courting mates, some of these birds prefer to dance in trees while others like to create a stage on the forest floor.

Communication:

  • TThe bird of paradise will make loud calls for announcing their territory, advertising their location to potential mates, or sound alarms.
  • They may also make gestures such as the beating of their wings or rattling of their bills.

Life History Cycle & Breeding Behaviors:

  • Breeding for the bird of paradise occurs around April through December.
  • Only the females will attend to the nest.

Predators:

  • Natural predators of the bird of paradise include snakes or larger birds like hawks.
